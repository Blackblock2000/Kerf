ISSUE 07 · SUNDAY 19 JULY 2026 · 8 MIN READ

HBM is sold out. The market is pricing the wrong node.

Verdict first: this week's memory strength is real, but the binding constraint has moved one node downstream — advanced packaging is where 2027 allocation gets decided, and it is the least crowded expression of the same fact.

What happened. Three signals hit the memory node this week: HBM4 qualification pulled forward at two suppliers, hyperscaler commentary repeating that memory — not logic — is the shortage, and spot conventional DRAM tightening as HBM crowds out wafer capacity. The memory basket outran SOX by 190bps in five sessions. Everyone saw it.

Propagation · this week's dominant path
Memory & HBM→ Equipment→ Adv. Packaging→ Integration

The mechanism. Sold-out HBM does not transmit value evenly. Each incremental stack consumes interposer area, bonder time, and test capacity that conventional parts never needed — so the marginal euro of memory demand is increasingly spent at the packaging node, where bookings this week quietly extended into 2028. Packaging pricing firms when it is the queue, not the quote, that rations supply. That is the regime the chain entered this month.

The second-order read. Friday's Kimi K3 release matters here for one reason: it hardens the case that Chinese domestic compute capex continues regardless of who wins the frontier race, and those accelerator programmes are, if anything, more packaging-intensive per unit of compute…

The names carrying that exposure divide into three groups. The first — obvious TSMC-adjacent capacity — is well held and fairly priced. The second, the OSAT tier, is where queue-pricing does the most work on margins, and where this week's Taiwanese trade press…

Alert · 15 Jul 2026 · 16:42 London

BIS proposes expanded end-use controls on advanced packaging equipment

What. A proposed rule in the Federal Register extends end-use controls to a defined class of advanced packaging tools, with a 45-day comment period. It is a proposal, not a final rule.

Policy→ Equipment→ Adv. Packaging

Where it lands. Equipment first — order visibility for affected tool classes, and the licensing overhead that comes with it. Packaging second, via the capacity that those tools were destined to build.

What it touches. Tool vendors with China-destined packaging exposure; OSATs with expansion plans dependent on the affected classes. The chain map on the site is updated.

Source: Federal Register, public. This is information, not advice.

Method

How this is made

Sources

Everything here derives from public sources: company investor-relations releases and exchange filings, regulator publications (BIS, EU, METI equivalents), and trade press across English, Traditional Chinese, Korean and Japanese — machine-translated and human-checked. Every item links to its origin. Nothing comes from paid terminals, private datasets, or any employer's resources.

Pipeline

  • Feeds are polled hourly. Items pass a two-stage review: a fast relevance filter, then a stronger model that assigns a value-chain node, maps affected listed companies, and drafts a neutral two-line summary.
  • Classification disagreements are flagged for human review before publication. Corrections are logged and applied on the next run.
  • The weekly brief is written by a human. The pipeline supplies raw material, not judgment.
  • Prices are daily closes from public sources. Node baskets are equal-weighted and normalised from a stated baseline — a news-flow companion, not an index.

Independence

KERF is a personal project, written in a personal capacity. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced using the resources of the author's employer. Names appear in neutral data displays because they belong to the value chain; inclusion is structural, not a view.
